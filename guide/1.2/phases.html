Phases

With phases, every element remembers the phase it is built in and the phase it is taken down in. One model then gives the existing drawings, the demolition drawings and the proposed drawings, and a view shows the project as it is at one moment.

The one idea to keep in mind: a view stands at a phase. What it shows, and what you draw or delete in it, follow from that phase.

Start phases

  1. Open the Phases tab of Properties.
  2. Say what you have drawn so far:
    • It is the existing building: everything already drawn is Existing.
    • It is the proposed building: everything already drawn is new, built in Proposed.

The project gets three phases, first to last: Existing, Demolition (a phase that takes things down) and Proposed.

Move a view through time

The tag beside a view's name (Existing ▾) is the phase the view stands at. Click it and pick another phase. The Phases list in the Phases tab (its View line at the top names the view it speaks for) and View tab › Phase › View at do the same thing.

The tag is grey at the first phase, the demolition colour at a phase that takes things down, and green at one that builds.

Tip: The 3D view stands at a phase of its own, set from its own tag.

1. Draw what is there: stand at Existing

Stand the view at Existing and draw the building as surveyed. Everything you draw here is built in Existing.

A delete here is a real delete: the element was built in this very phase, so it was never there. It does not draw as demolished. Ctrl+Z brings it back.

2. Take things down: stand at Demolition

Stand the view at Demolition, select what goes and press Delete. It is taken down: it stays in the model, and

  • a view standing at Demolition draws it in red dashes;
  • a view standing at Proposed leaves it out;
  • a view standing at Existing still shows it, as it was.

A wall taken down takes its doors and windows with it. Openings left in walls that stay are listed under Taken down as making good, with the area to fill.

Tip: No red dashes? Look at the tag. The dashes show only in a view standing at the phase that takes the element down. If you deleted it at Existing, it was deleted, not taken down: undo, step to Demolition and delete it there.

3. Draw what is new: stand at Proposed

Stand the view at Proposed and draw the new work. It is built in Proposed. Existing work draws fainter behind it, so the new stands out.

Deleting existing work from Proposed takes it down in the latest phase before it that takes things down (Demolition).

Bring something back

What a phase took down can come back, without undoing anything drawn since:

  • Phases tab › Taken down: click one or more rows, then Bring back. Bring all brings everything back.
  • Or select the element in a view standing at Demolition, then Element tab › Phase › Bring back.

It stands again in every phase, with the doors and windows that came down with it.

Change existing work from a later phase

Moving or editing an existing wall from a view at Proposed asks what you mean. Phases tab › Existing work › Changes:

  • Correct: the survey was wrong. The element changes in every phase.
  • Alter: the building is being changed. The old one comes down and the changed one is built in the phase the view stands at. Ctrl+Z twice puts it back.

An element's phases

Select an element: Element tab › Phase shows

RowWhat it says
Built inthe phase it is built in
Down inthe phase it is taken down in, or Never
In viewwhat it is in the view in front: as surveyed, existing, to take down, new in … or not here

Change Built in or Down in to move an element in time without redrawing it.

How they draw

Phases tab › How they draw sets the office's convention for every view, sheet and the 3D view:

  • Down: the colour of what comes down, and Dashed.
  • Existing › Fainter: what was built before the view's phase, lighter and without hatching.
  • New › One colour: everything a phase builds in one colour.

Reset goes back to red dashes, existing fainter, new in its own colours.

Your own phases

Click a phase in the list to stand the view at it, then under the list: Name, Takes down, + Phase (a new one after it), Earlier and Later, and Remove (only an empty phase).

Sheets for a planning application

Make a floor plan view for each phase (duplicate the plan) and stand each at a phase: Existing, Demolition, Proposed. Place the three on a sheet side by side.

Options

Options in the Phases tab hold design questions with two or more answers (Option A, Option B). A view shows one option and works in it, from its tag's menu. See the tag's Working in list and Ghost the other options.
